How to use this book

Every scenario is written as a full call — covering every Setter/Closer checkpoint (right person, accident date, the story, fault, police report/insurance, injuries, treatment, property damage) the way a real caller would describe it. The Nuance zooms in on the one detail that determines the outcome, and The Reason gives the disposition and the criteria behind it. Many scenarios are paired or grouped so you can see the exact fact that flips an otherwise identical story. Turn on Quiz Mode to read the story and nuance, decide your own disposition, then reveal the answer.

Clean Rear-End, All Checkpoints Clear

QUALIFIED
Scenario

The caller is Maria Lopez, calling about her own accident — right person. The accident happened 9 days ago on a Tuesday evening. Maria was driving home from work and stopped at a red light on a four-lane road. She had been stopped for several seconds when she felt a hard impact from behind. The other driver, in a Toyota Camry, admitted fault on the spot — "I didn't see you stop in time." A police officer responded, took both statements, and cited the other driver for following too closely. Maria has his insurance card — State Farm, confirmed active. The Nuance

Every checkpoint lines up cleanly — but the rep should still walk through all eight rather than stopping early because "rear-end + admitted fault" sounds done. QUALIFIEDThe Reason: QUALIFIED. Maria is the plaintiff and not at fault (defendant admitted fault, cited by police). Defendant has active insurance to pursue, and Maria's own policy was active (Prop 213 satisfied). Injury and treatment within 30 days are confirmed (next-day urgent care, ongoing chiropractic). Property damage (caved bumper, broken trunk latch) supports the claim.

QUALIFIEDThe Reason: QUALIFIED. Rear corner damage from a defendant who was behind Ben and swerved is explicitly the criteria's described pattern for rear-end accidents — not a lane dispute. 1.8

Two Separate Impacts: PC Hit the Front Car First, Then Was Rear-Ended

ESCALATE
Scenario

The caller is Marcus Webb, the driver — right person. The accident happened 6 days ago. Marcus describes it carefully: "I actually tapped the car in front of me first — I was looking down for like a second and bumped them, no big deal, light contact. Then maybe 10-15 seconds later, before either of us could even pull over, someone behind me hit me hard." Two separate, sequential impacts. The rear driver was cited for following too closely; his insurance (Allstate) is active. Marcus has neck pain, saw a chiropractor 5 days later. The Nuance

This is structurally different from 1.7 — there, ONE impact (from behind) pushed Felicia into the car ahead. Here, Marcus describes TWO separate, sequential impacts: he caused the first one (tapping the car ahead while not looking), and then was struck from behind by someone else entirely. Two impacts mean two separate liability pictures, and injury causation between them becomes difficult to untangle.

ESCALATEThe Reason: ESCALATE. Only the second impact (the rear-end Marcus suffered) is potentially viable as Marcus's claim — and the front car's claim against Marcus for the first impact is legitimate, since Marcus caused that one. An attorney needs to evaluate whether the case is worth taking given the split liability and the difficulty of separating which injuries came from which impact.

Defendant's Unprotected Left, PC T-Bones the Defendant

Scenario

The caller is Angela Brooks, the injured driver — right person. The accident happened 11 days ago around 6 PM. Lane Merges & Sideswipes

5 scenarios
3.1

Defendant Merging Into PC's Established Lane

QUALIFIED
Scenario

The caller is Priya Anand, the driver — right person. The accident happened 2 weeks ago around 8 AM during the morning commute. Priya was driving in the right lane of a three-lane highway, had been in that lane for at least a mile, not changing lanes, doing the speed of traffic. A car in the middle lane began merging right into Priya's lane — "He just drifted over, I think he didn't see me, there was no signal." The merging car's rear quarter panel struck the front driver's side of Priya's car. No independent witness, no dashcam. No police report — both drivers pulled to the shoulder and exchanged information; the other driver has Allstate, claim "under review." Priya has neck pain and a sore wrist; saw her PCP 4 days later (X-ray negative), started PT a week after that. Front bumper and driver's side fender dented, wheel well scraped. Priya's own policy was active.

The Nuance

No witness, no dashcam, no police report — on paper this looks like the "tricky lane merge, can't prove it" pattern reps fear. But the criteria's actual rule is: unless the PC's OWN story places them at fault (i.e., the PC was merging), we can sign. Priya was established in her lane for over a mile; the OTHER driver was merging. The independent-witness requirement is for disputes where it's unclear who was established and who was moving — not this case.

QUALIFIEDThe Reason: QUALIFIED. Priya's own account places the defendant as the merging vehicle drifting into her established lane — her story does not place her at fault. Defendant insurance is reviewing the claim; Priya's own policy was active (Prop 213 satisfied). Injury and treatment within 30 days confirmed.

QUALIFIED
Scenario

A rep takes a call. The caller describes an accident — any type, any state, any injury level. Before reaching for "qualified" or "not qualified," the rep should be able to answer four questions, regardless of how the story was told: (1) Is the PC the plaintiff and not at fault — based on their own story, the police report, and any independent evidence? (2) Is the PC injured, did they begin treatment within the required window for that case type (30 days for auto/ped/bike/motorcycle; same/next day for slip & fall; first few days for premise), and — if the case is older — have they avoided gaps of 30+ days? (3) Is there insurance to PURSUE — defendant coverage, or the PC's UM if the defendant has none (and only if the PC is not at fault)? And (4) — for California auto cases specifically — was the PC DRIVER themselves an insured driver on the date of loss (Prop 213)?

The Nuance

Checks 3 and 4 are both "insurance," but they answer different questions: #3 is "is there money on the other side," #4 is "is the PC even eligible to recover under CA law." A case can pass #3 — the defendant is fully insured and has accepted liability — and still fail #4 if the PC driver had no policy of their own on the DOL. Confirming "the other guy has insurance" answers #3 only; it says nothing about #4. Every CA auto case with a PC driver needs both confirmed separately.

QUALIFIEDThe Reason: A case is QUALIFIED only when ALL FOUR checks hold AND no case-type exception applies on top of them — household passenger exceptions (spouse/minor child/person you live with), government immunity and SOL clocks (6 months CA, 2 years NV), federal defendant exclusions (USPS), big-injury requirements (government claims, city/sidewalk, bus-as-defendant, places of entertainment, non-bite dog injuries), state-specific minor rules (CA/NV include regardless of injury with a signing injured adult; CO requires injury+treatment; WA excludes under-17 soft tissue), and venue-specific exclusions (CA casinos). When a single fact is missing — UM status unconfirmed, release scope unclear, intersection arrow unverified — the disposition is CONDITIONAL until that fact is obtained. When the criteria itself says to consult (catastrophic injury/death on an at-fault left turn, big-injury gap exceptions, places of entertainment, approaching SOLs), the disposition is ESCALATE — never a solo rep-level reject OR sign in those situations.
